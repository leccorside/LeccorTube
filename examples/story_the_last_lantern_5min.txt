[TITLE: The Last Lantern | A Short Story About Courage]
[THUMBNAIL: She Kept The Light]
[CAST: Noor = a 12 year old girl, long dark brown hair in a single braid, brave dark eyes, light brown skin, dark green raincoat, yellow rubber boots]
[CAST: Dawood = a 70 year old man, short white beard, kind wrinkled face, grey wool sweater, dark blue knitted cap, walks with a wooden cane]
[CAST: Yusuf = a 40 year old fisherman, short black beard, strong tired face, orange waterproof jacket]

# The Keeper

[IMG: an old white lighthouse on a rocky cliff above a grey sea, small stone cottage beside it, seagulls, early morning]
On the edge of a small fishing village, where the land ended and the sea began, there stood an old white lighthouse.

[IMG: @Dawood climbing a spiral stone staircase inside a lighthouse, holding a cane, warm lamp light from above]
For fifty years, it had been kept by one man. His name was Dawood. Every evening, he climbed one hundred and twelve steps to light the great lamp at the top.

[IMG: @Noor sitting on the stairs of the lighthouse, watching @Dawood polish a huge glass lens, golden light]
His granddaughter Noor followed him every night. She counted the steps out loud, and she watched him polish the giant glass lens until it shone like the sun.

@Noor: Grandfather, why do you light it every single night, even when the sea is calm?
@Dawood: Because the sea is only calm until it is not. And somewhere out there, someone is always trying to find their way home.

[IMG: @Yusuf pushing a small wooden fishing boat into the sea at dawn, @Noor waving from the shore]
Noor's father, Yusuf, was a fisherman. Every morning, before the sun was up, his little blue boat left the harbor. And every evening, Noor watched for it from the window at the top of the tower.

# The Storm

[IMG: dark storm clouds rolling over the sea, huge waves crashing against rocks, lightning in the distance, dramatic]
One autumn evening, the sky turned the color of iron. The wind began to howl, and the waves grew tall and angry.

[IMG: @Noor pressing her hands against a rain-covered window at the top of a lighthouse, looking out at a stormy sea, worried face]
Noor searched the water for the little blue boat. It was not there.

[IMG: @Dawood lying on a wooden floor at the bottom of spiral stairs, holding his ankle in pain, @Noor kneeling beside him, lantern light]
Then she heard a crash. Her grandfather had slipped on the wet stairs. He could not stand.

@Dawood: Noor, listen to me. The lamp must be lit. Your father is out there.

[IMG: close-up of @Noor looking up the dark spiral staircase, frightened but determined eyes, rain dripping from her braid]
Noor looked up at the dark staircase. One hundred and twelve steps. She had never climbed them alone. She had never lit the lamp herself.

@Noor: I can do it, Grandfather. I have watched you a thousand times.

# The Light

[IMG: @Noor running up a spiral stone staircase in the dark, holding a small oil lantern, wind blowing through a cracked window]
She took the small oil lantern and began to climb. The wind screamed through the cracks in the old walls. The tower shook with every wave.

[IMG: @Noor standing in the dark lamp room of a lighthouse, a huge glass lens in front of her, rain lashing the windows, her lantern glowing]
At the top, the lamp room was cold and black. Noor struck a match. The wind blew it out. She struck another. Out again.

[IMG: close-up of @Noor's hands shielding a small match flame with her raincoat, glowing orange light on her face]
Her hands were shaking. Then she remembered her grandfather's words. Someone is always trying to find their way home.

[IMG: @Noor pulling off her green raincoat and holding it up as a shield against the wind, lighting the big lamp, determined face]
She took off her raincoat and held it up like a wall against the wind. She struck the last match, and slowly, carefully, she touched it to the wick.

[IMG: a huge beam of golden light bursting from the top of a lighthouse into a dark stormy night, rain, epic wide shot]
The flame caught. The great lens came alive, and a beam of golden light swept out across the storm.

[IMG: @Yusuf in a small blue fishing boat in huge dark waves at night, looking up at a distant beam of lighthouse light, hope on his face]
Far out at sea, in the middle of the dark, a tired fisherman lifted his head. He saw the light. And he turned his little blue boat toward home.

# Morning

[IMG: calm sea at sunrise, soft pink and gold sky, a small blue fishing boat tied at a wooden dock, peaceful]
When the sun rose, the sea was quiet again, as if nothing had happened.

[IMG: @Yusuf hugging @Noor tightly on a wooden dock at sunrise, both with tears of joy, warm golden light]
Yusuf ran up the path and lifted his daughter into his arms.

@Yusuf: I was lost, Noor. I could not see anything. And then, there was your light.

[IMG: @Dawood sitting in a chair with a bandaged ankle, smiling proudly at @Noor, cozy cottage with morning light through the window]
Her grandfather smiled from his chair, his ankle wrapped in a bandage.

@Dawood: The lighthouse has a new keeper now.

[IMG: @Noor climbing the lighthouse spiral staircase at sunset, holding a lantern, confident small smile, warm golden light]
From that night on, it was Noor who climbed the one hundred and twelve steps every evening.

[IMG: the white lighthouse shining its beam over a calm sea at night under a sky full of stars, peaceful wide shot]
And she never forgot what the storm had taught her. Being brave does not mean you are not afraid. It means you light the lamp anyway.
