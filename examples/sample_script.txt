# The Village Before Dawn
[IMG: a small medieval English village before dawn, thatched cottages, low mist over the fields, a single lit window]
Long before the sun rose over the hills, the village was already stirring. A rooster called once, then fell silent again, as if it had changed its mind. Smoke began to curl from a few chimneys. In the cottage at the end of the lane, a man named Thomas pulled a rough wool blanket from his shoulders and sat up in the dark.

He did not need a clock. Nobody in the village owned one. The body simply knew, after a lifetime of the same mornings, when it was time to rise. He felt for his boots beside the hearth and pushed his feet into them, still stiff with yesterday's mud.

[IMG: interior of a dark medieval peasant cottage, glowing embers in a stone hearth, clay pots, straw on the floor]
The fire had burned down to a soft orange glow. Thomas knelt and blew on the embers, gently, the way his father had shown him. A small flame rose. He fed it a handful of dry straw and then a split piece of oak, and the room slowly filled with a warm, flickering light.

# The Fields
[IMG: medieval farmer leading two oxen through a misty field at sunrise, wooden plough, soft golden light]
Outside, the air was cold and smelled of wet earth. Thomas walked the short path to the shared fields, where the land was divided into long narrow strips. Each family worked its own strips, scattered here and there, so that no one received only the best soil or only the worst.

His oxen were waiting, patient as always, their breath rising in clouds. He spoke to them quietly as he fastened the yoke. Ploughing was slow work. The wooden blade cut a shallow line through the soil, and then another, and then another, while the sun climbed higher and the mist began to lift.

# Evening in the Village
[IMG: medieval village at dusk, candlelight in small windows, a dog sleeping by a wooden door, stars appearing]
By the time the light began to fade, every muscle in his back was tired. Thomas walked home slowly. The lane was quiet now. A dog lay curled beside a doorway, and somewhere an owl called from the edge of the woods.

Inside, his wife had kept a pot of pottage warm over the fire, thick with beans and a few leaves of cabbage. They ate in comfortable silence. Then the candle was pinched out, the fire banked for the night, and the small cottage settled into darkness, like the rest of the sleeping village.
